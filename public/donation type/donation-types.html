Types of Blood Donation

Discover the different ways you can donate blood and help save lives. Each type serves unique medical needs.

Whole Blood Donation

The most common and simplest type of blood donation. During a whole blood donation, approximately one pint (about 470ml) of blood is collected. The donation itself takes about 8-10 minutes, while the entire process (including registration, health screening, and recovery) takes about an hour.

Used For:

  • Trauma patients and accident victims
  • Surgery patients (both planned and emergency procedures)
  • Patients with anemia and blood disorders
  • Cancer patients undergoing treatment
  • Patients with blood loss during childbirth

Requirements:

  • Donors must weigh at least 50kg (110 lbs)
  • Must be at least 18 years old (16-17 with parental consent in some regions)
  • Must wait 56 days between donations
  • Must be in good general health and feeling well

Plasma Donation

Plasma donation (plasmapheresis) collects the liquid portion of your blood (plasma). During the donation, blood is drawn from one arm and passed through a high-tech machine that separates the plasma, then returns your red cells and platelets to you along with some saline. The process takes about 1.5 hours.

Used For:

  • Burn victims and trauma patients
  • Patients with severe bleeding disorders
  • People with immune deficiencies
  • Research and pharmaceutical uses
  • Patients undergoing organ transplants

Requirements:

  • Donors must weigh at least 50kg (110 lbs)
  • Must be at least 18 years old
  • Can donate every 28 days, up to 13 times per year
  • Must have adequate vein size for the apheresis machine
  • AB blood type is especially valuable for plasma donation

Platelets Donation

Platelet donation (plateletpheresis) collects just your platelets. Like plasma donation, blood is drawn from one arm and passed through a machine that separates the platelets, then returns your red cells and most of the plasma back to you. The process takes about 2-2.5 hours.

Used For:

  • Cancer patients undergoing chemotherapy
  • Patients with leukemia or aplastic anemia
  • Organ transplant recipients
  • Patients undergoing major surgery
  • Patients with platelet function disorders

Requirements:

  • Donors must weigh at least 50kg (110 lbs)
  • Must be at least 18 years old
  • Can donate every 7 days, up to 24 times per year
  • Must avoid aspirin for 48 hours before donation
  • A, B, and AB blood types are especially valuable for platelet donation

Double Red Cells Donation

Double red cell donation (erythrocytapheresis) allows you to safely donate two units of red blood cells in one appointment. This automated process collects your red blood cells and returns most of the plasma and platelets to you. The donation takes about 1.5 hours.

Used For:

  • Trauma patients and accident victims
  • Surgery patients with significant blood loss
  • Patients with chronic anemia
  • Patients with sickle cell disease
  • Newborns who need blood transfusions

Requirements:

  • Male donors must be at least 5'1" and weigh at least 59kg (130 lbs)
  • Female donors must be at least 5'5" and weigh at least 68kg (150 lbs)
  • Must be at least 18 years old
  • Must wait 112 days between donations
  • O, A-, and B- blood types are especially valuable for double red donation

Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers to common questions about blood donation and eligibility.

The frequency depends on the type of donation:

  • Whole Blood: Every 56 days (about every 2 months)
  • Plasma: Every 28 days (about once a month)
  • Platelets: Every 7 days, up to 24 times per year
  • Double Red Cells: Every 112 days (about every 4 months)

To prepare for your donation:

  • Get a good night's sleep before donating
  • Eat a healthy meal rich in iron (like red meat, fish, or spinach)
  • Drink plenty of fluids (water or juice) before and after donation
  • Avoid fatty foods before donating (they can affect tests)
  • Bring your ID and list of any medications you're taking

The time varies by donation type:

  • Whole Blood: About 1 hour total (10-15 minutes for actual donation)
  • Plasma: About 1.5 hours total (45-60 minutes for actual donation)
  • Platelets: About 2-2.5 hours total (90-120 minutes for actual donation)
  • Double Red Cells: About 1.5 hours total (45-60 minutes for actual donation)

This includes registration, health screening, donation, and recovery time.

Most people feel fine after donating, but some temporary side effects may include:

  • Minor bruising at the needle site
  • Feeling lightheaded or dizzy
  • Fatigue
  • Nausea

These effects usually resolve quickly. To minimize side effects:

  • Drink extra fluids for 24 hours after donating
  • Avoid strenuous activity for 5-6 hours
  • Eat iron-rich foods to replenish your iron stores

Proper nutrition helps ensure a successful donation and quick recovery:

Before Donation:

  • Iron-rich foods (red meat, spinach, beans, iron-fortified cereals)
  • Vitamin C-rich foods (oranges, tomatoes) to help iron absorption
  • Plenty of water (at least 16 oz extra before donation)
  • Avoid fatty foods which can affect blood tests

After Donation:

  • Continue drinking extra fluids for 24-48 hours
  • Eat iron-rich meals for the next few weeks
  • Include protein sources to help replenish plasma proteins
  • Avoid alcohol for 24 hours as it can lead to dehydration

Exercise after blood donation requires some precautions:

  • First 24 hours: Avoid strenuous exercise or heavy lifting
  • First 5 hours: No vigorous activity at all
  • Light activities: Walking and gentle stretching are fine
  • Hydration: Drink extra fluids if doing any exercise
  • Listen to your body: Stop if you feel lightheaded or fatigued

For platelet or plasma donations, you may resume normal activities after resting, but still avoid intense workouts for 24 hours. Athletes should wait 2-3 days before competitive training or events.
